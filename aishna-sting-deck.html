01 / Vision

AI assistants should help people cooperate, not only automate alone.

As assistants become tool-using representatives, the durable layer should be the person's intent, boundaries, agreements and audit trail, not a single closed AI platform.

02 / Problem

Useful agent work stops at human and platform boundaries.

AI assistants can search, write, reason and operate tools, but they lack a simple open way to discover mutual benefit with another person's assistant, respect boundaries, negotiate acceptable action and leave evidence both people can inspect.

03 / Why Now

Agent interoperability is arriving before human-centered trust is solved.

Tool-using assistants, MCP-style integrations and agent-to-agent protocols are becoming normal. The missing startup opportunity is the cooperation layer around consent, provenance, receipts and portable declarations for ordinary people and teams.

04 / Solution

Aishna lets assistants cooperate through declared human terms.

A person declares needs, offers and boundaries. Their assistant can discover compatible opportunities across public interfaces. Actions leave auditable records so humans can review what happened.

person -> assistant -> declared needs/offers/boundaries -> another person or agent -> negotiated action -> receipt
05 / Hard To Copy

The edge is the combination, not one screen.

Protocol thinking: portable declarations, mutual-benefit constraints, identity boundaries and receipts.
Execution proof: live apps, MCP/API/auth/provenance pieces and experiments across different assistant contexts.
06 / Market

Start with AI-heavy operators; expand into trust infrastructure for delegated action.

Initial users are founders, developers, researchers, consultants and organizations already using assistants but blocked when work requires another person, organization or platform. The larger market is the trust, identity and provenance layer for agent-mediated work.

07 / Business Model

Validate paid pilots before committing to the final model.

  • Paid pilots with AI-heavy teams or innovation environments.
  • Hosted trust/provenance layer for agent-mediated workflows.
  • Premium tooling for declarations, matching, receipts, governance and analytics.
  • Protocol/community layer that can later support SaaS, enterprise or infrastructure partnerships.
08 / Go To Market

One focused cohort before broad launch.

  1. Interview 25-30 AI-heavy users.
  2. Identify the strongest boundary problem.
  3. Run a small cooperation trial with declared needs, offers and boundaries.
  4. Convert the clearest use case into a paid pilot or hosted experiment.

10 / Competitive Landscape

Most agent interoperability starts with software. Aishna starts with the principal.

A2A-style and open agent protocols focus on how agents discover and communicate. Aishna focuses on the person represented by the agent: declared intent, consent, mutual benefit, auditability and cooperation across independently chosen assistants.
