Scene

Early morning Baltic coast.

Not dramatic weather.

Not a storm.

Not a crisis.

Just a calm sea and light fog.

The fog is beginning to lift.

As it clears, important things become visible:

a navigation marker
shoreline contours
shallow rocks beneath the surface
a safe channel through the water

Nothing changes.

Only visibility changes.

---

Create a photorealistic editorial landscape photograph of the Baltic Sea coast at first light.

The image is not about weather, drama, beauty, or wilderness.

The image is about visibility.

A calm Baltic coastal navigation channel stretches into the distance. The sea is quiet. The shoreline is modest and realistic. No mountains, no cliffs, no fantasy scenery.

A thin layer of morning haze remains over the water, but the important elements are becoming visible:

navigation markers
channel buoys
shoreline contours
shallow underwater rocks visible beneath the surface
the safe passage through the water

Nothing in the scene is changing.

The rocks were always there.
The channel was always there.
The hazards were always there.

Only visibility is improving.

Composition should communicate discovery rather than transformation.

Approximately 70% of the image should be clear and legible.
Approximately 30% should still fade into soft haze.

The viewer should feel:

"I can finally see what I am dealing with."

No people.
No boats.
No text.
No symbols.
No glowing effects.
No concentric circles.
No futuristic elements.
No spiritual or mindfulness aesthetics.

Style:
Premium documentary photography.
Nordic coastal realism.
Natural color palette.

Decision-making becomes possible because reality becomes visible.

Mood:
Not peace.
